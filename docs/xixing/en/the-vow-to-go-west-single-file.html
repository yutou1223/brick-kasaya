The Vow to Go West The Moon over Chang'an · A Sentence Beneath the Big Wild Goose Pagoda

Some roads must be walked by someone first.

Xuanzang Big Wild Goose Pagoda India The Vow Moonlight

Outside the walls of Chang'an, beneath the Big Wild Goose Pagoda.

A monk stood before the statue of the Buddha. For a long, long while, he did not move.

The wind came from the west. It stirred his red kasaya, and the bronze bells along the eaves. He lifted his head, looked into the Buddha's compassionate eyes, and said one sentence, very softly:

If the Buddha will not go east, then I shall go west.

“Master, where are you going?”the young novice beside him did not understand

“To India. To bring back the true scriptures.”he said

“But that road crosses deserts and climbs over snow mountains — tens of thousands of li. Many set out, and never came back.”the novice grew anxious

The monk smiled. From his sleeve he took a worn and broken scroll, opened it, and pointed at the words. “Look — a line is missing here, a passage there. The scriptures we recite are incomplete. The Buddhist canon came from the west, passed from hand to hand along the way; it is like a bowl of water carried from far away — by the time it reaches us, most of it has spilled.”

He closed the scroll and put it back in his sleeve.

“Since the Buddha will not come to the east, I will go to the west. I will carry that bowl of water back again.”

That night he packed his bags, took his horse by the lead, and slipped quietly out of the city. He did not look back.

The lamps of the Big Wild Goose Pagoda shrank behind him, bit by bit, until they were like a star that had fallen to earth.

He walked west. The moon went before him, like a lamp lit for his sake.

He did not know what he would meet on the road, but he knew this — some roads must be walked by someone first.

Culture Notes

The monk on this phone case is Xuanzang, a master of the Tang dynasty. When he said “If the Buddha will not go east, then I shall go west,” he meant that he would not wait for the dharma to arrive in the east by itself — he would go west and seek the true scriptures himself.
The Big Wild Goose Pagoda was built in Chang'an under Xuanzang's supervision after his return from India, to house the scriptures and Buddha images he had brought back. He travelled for four years and fifty thousand li, returned with more than six hundred Buddhist texts, and spent the rest of his life translating them into Chinese.
Later, his story was written into Journey to the West. But the real Xuanzang had no Monkey King and no white dragon horse — only his own two feet, and the resolve in one sentence: “I shall go west.”
