A Little Wish in the Clouds Guardian of Tang · A Smile Above Cloud and Wave

Don't be afraid. I am here.

Guardian of Tang Ci'en Temple Red Kasaya Jade Waves Little Wish

The mornings of Chang'an are always woken by a bell that carries a long way.

Around the summit of the pagoda at the Great Ci'en Temple the mist coils like a staircase to the sky. Above the sea of cloud stands a little monk, palms pressed together, eyes closed, his expression devout and utterly calm.

His name is Xuanzang — and yet in this moment he looks more like a small deity keeping watch over this land.

Behind him rise the golden eaves of palace roofs, the emblem of the Tang at its height; beneath his feet roll the jade-green waves — the spray of the Eastern Sea in legend, and a miniature of the eighty-one trials on the road for the scriptures.

But he is not afraid.

Look: the corners of his mouth turn gently upward, and a faint blush touches his cheeks — the face of a heart full of hope. His red kasaya burns like a flame, striking against the sea of cloud. That red was among the most honoured colours of the Tang, standing for good fortune and courage.

He holds no weapon — only a string of prayer beads.

What is he praying for?

Perhaps for favourable weather and good harvests, perhaps for safety on far roads — or perhaps he is simply offering a small blessing to everyone who passes by.

On this phone case we are not looking at an ascetic, but at a “guardian” gently wrapped in Tang culture.

When you pick up your phone and see him smiling calmly amid cloud and wave, it is as if he is telling you:

It is as if he says However the waves of life may surge, so long as your heart stays as soft and as steady as a cloud, everything will come right.

Culture Notes

The little monk on this phone case has as his original the Tang master Xuanzang. But he is no longer the ascetic toiling across the desert — he has become a “guardian in the clouds”, full of hope.
His red kasaya was one of the most honoured colours of the Tang dynasty, a sign of good fortune and courage; his palms-pressed-together posture is the classic gesture of blessing in Eastern culture, standing for inner calm and devotion.
The golden eaves in the background take their cue from the Big Wild Goose Pagoda of the Tang — the scripture pagoda Xuanzang himself supervised after returning with the sutras, and still a landmark of Xi'an. The jade-green waves at his feet recall the legend of the Tongtian River in Journey to the West, and stand too for the rises and challenges of a life's road.

But look at him: a smile at the corner of his mouth, a blush on his cheeks, as if to say, “Don't be afraid. I am here.”
